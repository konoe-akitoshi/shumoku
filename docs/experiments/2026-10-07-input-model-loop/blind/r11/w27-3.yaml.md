1. The AS numbers themselves are not represented as network ownership or autonomous-system attributes.
2. I treated the unnamed AS64510 router and AS64540 as nodes, inferred that the AS64520 and AS64530 addresses belong to routers, and used `assumed` for the unnamed router and the unknown AS64540 path. I also assumed the AS64510 cable is Ethernet and represented its eBGP session as the cable link’s connection. I modeled IX-T as a routing domain and the peering LAN as a segment within it.
3. The format does not make clear how to represent a provider or exchange as an organization distinct from a router, how to associate an AS number with nodes or sessions, or how to represent a peering session that runs over a cable or shared segment. It also leaves unclear whether `assumed` applies to the existence of a link when only its path is unknown.
