1. The format does not directly express that AS64510 is our transit provider and we are its customer, or that AS64540 transit is purchased. The connection labels and description retain some of that meaning.
2. I added `as64510-router`, `as64520-router`, `as64530-router`, and `as64540-router` as chosen IDs because the format needs node references for these peers. I marked them assumed because their names and existence as distinct nodes are not confirmed. I treated AS64530’s `.30` address as a router on the peering LAN, based on the description.
3. It is unclear whether `virtual: true` is appropriate for an eBGP session that runs over a cable: it describes a non-cable connection, while the link here represents the session and the cable details together. The 10G cable is stated, but its other endpoint is an unnamed router, and the format has no separate field for “session runs over this cable.”
